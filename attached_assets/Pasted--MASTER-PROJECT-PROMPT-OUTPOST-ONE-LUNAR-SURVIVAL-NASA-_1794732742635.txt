### NASA Space Apps Challenge – Complete Game Vision & Development Specification

Act as a Senior Game Developer, Educational Game Designer, Space Science Researcher, UX/UI Designer, and Full-Stack Engineer.

**Core Concept:**

Players become commanders of a lunar outpost. Their responsibility is to maintain the outpost, manage interconnected resources, respond to unexpected emergencies, and complete scientific missions.

Every decision has a cost.

There is no universally perfect decision. Players must understand the situation, evaluate available options, and choose the most appropriate solution based on the current mission conditions.

The game is an educational simulation, not a real spacecraft control system and not a source of live NASA telemetry.

# PART 2 — THE PROBLEM WE IDENTIFIED

## The Educational Problem

Space STEM education often presents two extremes:

1. Space content can be too abstract, making it difficult for students to understand how real missions operate.
2. Technical explanations can be too complicated for younger learners.

Students may learn about spacecraft, astronauts, lunar exploration, and space missions, but they do not always experience the engineering decisions behind those missions.

They learn what happened, but not necessarily:

- Why engineers selected a particular solution.
- What alternatives were available.
- What resources were sacrificed.
- What risks were involved.
- How one decision affected other systems.
- Why engineering decisions involve trade-offs.

## The Gap

The missing element is an opportunity for students to experience engineering decision-making rather than simply reading about it.

The educational challenge is to make these complex decisions understandable, interactive, and meaningful.

# PART 3 — HOW WE THOUGHT ABOUT SOLVING THE PROBLEM

Do not treat the solution as an ordinary game idea.

Build the project around the following problem-solving methodology.

## Step 1: Identify the Learning Gap

We first identified that students needed more than theoretical explanations about space exploration.

They needed to understand how engineering decisions influence mission survival.

## Step 2: Rethink Traditional Learning

We asked:

"What if students could experience the decisions behind a space mission instead of only learning about its results?"

This led us to move from passive learning toward interactive, decision-based learning.

## Step 3: Choose a Meaningful Environment

We selected a simulated lunar outpost because lunar exploration introduces several interconnected engineering challenges:

- Limited resources.
- Extreme environmental conditions.
- Radiation exposure.
- Power generation and storage.
- Water management.
- Food production.
- Life-support requirements.
- Unexpected mission emergencies.

A lunar outpost provides a meaningful environment in which students can explore these challenges.

## Step 4: Connect the Resources

We designed a system in which five major resources influence one another:

1. Oxygen.
2. Water.
3. Power.
4. Food.
5. Radiation protection.

These resources should not feel like independent game statistics.

They represent interconnected systems that require careful management.

## Step 5: Introduce Real Engineering Trade-offs

We introduced decisions where each available option has advantages and disadvantages.

For example, a cooling-loop leak may require players to choose between repairing the system or taking another simulated emergency response.

Each option affects different resources and may influence future mission conditions.

## Step 6: Connect Decisions to Science

Every major event should explain:

- What happened.
- Why it happened.
- Which scientific concept explains it.
- What engineering challenge it creates.
- Why the selected response has certain consequences.

The goal is to ensure that gameplay itself becomes the learning experience.

## Final Design Philosophy

"Don't just teach students how space missions work. Let them experience why engineers make difficult decisions."
